## Not given: what the players want

The brief's `playersWant` is null. The Games Master did not say what the players want from this
character, and nobody guessed for them. Do not invent a want for the players. Where the instructions
above speak of what the players want, read it as the character's goodwill: what this person would
do for the players if they came to like them. This section decides how that changes each part of
the character. The brief still decides what is true, and the tone section that follows still
decides how it sounds.

- **The hook.** If the scene shows something this character wants done, a problem they have or a
  wrong they want put right, they offer it to the players: a job, a plea, a dare. That is the
  character's reason to talk to the players. If the scene shows no such thing, the character has no
  job to offer; do not make one up. What the scene leaves open stays a gap in square brackets, as
  the instructions above say.
- **`characterWants` is the twist.** It is true, and it sits beside the scene's hook, never in
  place of it. It is what the character is really after, under or alongside the job they offer.
- **`difficulty` is how hard they are to win over.** Easy: a kind word or a small favour does it.
  SomeWork: the players have to show they are worth it. Wall: they start cold and stay cold until
  the players do something that costs them.
- **`obstacle` is why their goodwill does not come free.** Wont: they are slow to warm to
  strangers, and say why. Cant: they would help, but what they can offer is smaller than it looks.
  ForAPrice: they warm up once they are paid, in goodwill or in coin; name the price.

The six parts of the output change like this:

1. **Who they are.** As above, with nothing about what the players want.
2. **Where they stand.** What they think of the players walking in, and what they want from them:
   the scene's hook if there is one, and the twist.
3. **How they seem, and what is underneath.** The surface they show strangers, then what the
   players would see once trusted.
4. **Levers.** Two or three ways to win their goodwill, each with a line of dialogue and what it
   earns: a better deal, the job, a tip, a door opened.
5. **Anti-levers.** Two or three ways to lose it, each with a line and what it costs: full price,
   turned away, an enemy made.
6. **Afterwards.** What they do once the players leave: won over / left as they were / made an
   enemy.

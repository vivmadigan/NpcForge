## Tone: Heartfelt

This character is written to be cared about. The tone must be unmistakable: someone reading only
your text should know it is heartfelt from the first two lines. Where the instructions above sound
cold, this section decides how it sounds. The brief still decides what is true.

- **Who they are.** The line of history is about someone or something they love, or have lost.
- **Where they stand.** Their position comes from caring about something: a person, a place, a
  promise. Say what, in concrete terms.
- **How they seem.** Show warmth on the surface: a kindness, a joke to put people at ease, an offer
  of food or rest. Underneath, show the hurt or the hope the brief points to, and let it be seen.
- **Levers and anti-levers.** The levers are about the heart: honesty, a shared loss, keeping
  someone safe, being treated as a person. The anti-levers are cruelty, contempt or carelessness
  with what they love. Their lines are open and personal.
- **Wall still means wall.** They refuse because of what they care about, and the refusal costs
  them. Let the players see that it hurts to say no.
- **Afterwards.** Each outcome changes them a little: gratitude, quiet grief, a door left open.

Write warm, gentle dialogue with small, human details. Avoid menace and cynicism. Avoid jokes at
the character's expense.

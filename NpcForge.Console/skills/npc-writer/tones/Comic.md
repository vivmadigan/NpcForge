## Tone: Comic

This character is played for laughs. The tone must be unmistakable: someone reading only your
text should know it is comic from the first two lines. Where the instructions above sound grave,
this section decides how it sounds. The brief still decides what is true.

- **Who they are.** The line of history is a comic one: a small, specific, absurd fact about them
  that the table will remember.
- **Where they stand.** Their position is real, and their reasons for it are ridiculous, petty or
  wildly out of proportion.
- **How they seem.** Give them one comic engine and run it hard: vanity, panic, pedantry, a strange
  obsession, total misplaced confidence. What is underneath can be true and still funny.
- **Levers and anti-levers.** Each one is a joke the players can play: flattery that works far too
  well, a bribe in exactly the wrong currency, a threat they take personally for the wrong reason.
  Every line of dialogue should get a laugh or a groan.
- **Wall still means wall.** They do not give way easily. Their stubbornness is the comedy:
  absurd excuses, escalating bargains, a refusal they defend with total conviction.
- **Afterwards.** End each outcome on a comic note, such as a grudge over something trivial or
  telling the story badly to everyone.

Write lively, specific, quotable dialogue. Avoid grim detail, menace and sorrow. Nothing here
should read as tense or sad.

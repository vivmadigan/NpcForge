**What this run is for.** The second of the three runs step 7's "Done when" asks for. It
turned out to be more than that: the obstacle rolled `ForAPrice` instead of `Wont`, so it is
the first test of whether the skill's per-obstacle rule does anything.

**The headline: the per-obstacle rule works.** `SKILL.md` says *"**ForAPrice** — they will,
once the price in `characterWants` is met. Name the price."* Mara names it, precisely and
unprompted: a reputable, expensive mason to seal the cellar niche, no questions, bring the
receipt. Run 02 at the same difficulty refused outright. Same `Wall`, two completely
different scenes — which is exactly what [README "How difficulty works"](../../README.md)
says the second roll exists to produce.

This is the objection the challenger raised against the first draft of step 7, now settled by
evidence. A rule phrased as "at a `Wall`, do not give it up" would have broken this character.

**Levers differ from run 02.** Different brief fields entirely:

| Lever 1 | Weakness — impressed by confidence | CharacterWants — secret buried |
| Lever 2 | CharacterWants — left alone | Obstacle — meet the price |
| Lever 3 | Attitude — nosy | Weakness — compelled to correct |

**Observed.**

- The wrong-about is not a separate ornament this time: *"believes expensive things are always
  better"* shapes the price itself — it must be an **expensive** mason, and cheap work is an
  anti-lever. The trait drives the scene rather than sitting in a bullet beside it.
- `CharacterWants` appears as both a lever and an anti-lever — seal the niche, or threaten to
  open it. The same want pulling both ways is good writing, though it means six bullets cover
  only five distinct brief entries.
- **The invented fields have converged three times now.** Merrin Voss / the Brass Lantern,
  Mara Venn / the Brass Lantern, Mara Vell / the Brass Kettle. "Mara" twice running, and
  **"Brass" in all three inn names.** The inn is the stronger tell. Nothing in the brief names
  either, which is the point: rolled traits diverge, invented ones collapse onto the model's
  favourites. Same pattern as the step 6 note (Merrit Vane, Sella Quill, Vell Marrow, Sella Vane).
- `attitude` rolled `nosy` again, same as run 02. That is the dice, not the model.

1. **Who they are** - **Mara Vell**, proprietor of the Brass Kettle, who inherited its cellar-and one old problem-from her late uncle.

2. **Where they stand** - Mara knows the fence's name and knows why strangers ask for it. She will not give it up for coin or threats: the name is tied to something she needs kept buried. Her price is plain once pressed: pay a reputable, expensive mason to seal the old niche in her cellar, ask no questions about what was in it, and bring her the mason's receipt.

3. **How they seem, and what is underneath** - She is briskly welcoming and relentlessly nosy, pouring ale while asking where the party slept last night, what they carry, and whether their boots came from outside the city. When the fence comes up, she laughs too quickly and corrects even small errors-"Not a *smuggler*, dear. There is a difference"-before changing the subject. Underneath, she is frightened that an old secret will surface, and has convinced herself that only the finest mason, at the highest price, can make the problem stay buried.

4. **Levers**
   - **CharacterWants: to keep a secret buried** - Offer to hire a mason and have the cellar niche sealed without inspecting it.
     *"Properly sealed? Mortar, stone, the lot? Then perhaps I can remember a name."*
   - **Obstacle: ForAPrice** - Meet the stated price: an expensive, respected mason's work and proof it was done.
     *"Not some alley-bricklayer with lime under his nails. Master Orven, or someone of that calibre. Bring me the receipt."*
   - **Weakness: feels compelled to correct people** - Deliberately get a detail about city trades or the underworld wrong, then let her set it straight.
     *"No, no-fences do not buy stolen goods; they arrange their sale. Honestly, how have you survived this long?"*

5. **Anti-levers**
   - **WillNotDiscuss: an embarrassing superstition** - Ask why the niche must be sealed, mention omens, or suggest there is something foolish behind her fear.
     *"It is damp, that is all. Cellars get damp. Don't stand there making faces at it."*
   - **CharacterWants: to keep a secret buried** - Threaten to search the cellar, expose her, or pry into the sealed space.
     *"You can leave the Brass Kettle now, or you can leave with the watch. Those are the choices."*
   - **WrongAbout: believes expensive things are always better** - Offer a cheap mason, bargain work, or insist any patch of mortar will do.
     *"Cheap work cracks. Cheap locks turn. Cheap promises rot before breakfast."*

6. **Afterwards**
   - **Got it:** Once the niche is sealed to her standards, Mara provides the fence's name, location, and the warning that the fence never deals twice with anyone who brings city watch attention.
   - **Didn't:** She keeps serving the party politely but gives them nothing beyond gossip, laughing nervously whenever they return to the subject.
   - **Made an enemy:** If they pry into the cellar or mock what she will not discuss, Mara quietly spreads word among innkeepers that they are trouble, making rooms and local cooperation markedly harder to find.

# Hessa Dunmore

**Keeper of the Sparrow & Pike, third generation, forty years old and has never slept a night outside this ward.**

---

## Where they stand

She knows the name. It's Sabin Marrow, who does his buying through the back of a glover's shop on Candle Row and drinks in her taproom on fifth-days because her beer is cheap and her memory is understood to be poor. She could say it in two seconds.

She won't. Not for coin — she has coin — and not for goodwill, because the thing she wants can't be bought from anyone but people like the players, and people like the players don't wander in every week.

Three months ago Rennick Pell, who keeps the chophouse across the way, measured her hanging sign, decided it overhung his frontage by four inches, and had the ward officers take it down. It has sat in her cellar since. Pell put up a bigger one of his own the following week. She thinks about this daily. She has thought about it while lying awake. **She wants Pell's sign down, in daylight, with Pell watching, and she wants him to understand why.** That is the price of Sabin Marrow's name, and she will not be talked down to a smaller one.

---

## How they seem, and what is underneath

Brisk, loud, hands going the whole time — she talks with a cloth in one fist and conducts with it, wipes a table she's already wiped, taps the bar twice to end a sentence. She tells people what she thinks of them in the fewest words available and doesn't soften it afterward. *"You're bleeding on my floor. Sit there, not there."*

Underneath she is a woman running one small vendetta with total seriousness and enjoying it more than she'd admit. She also believes she is a subtle and accomplished liar. She is not. When she lies her hands stop dead, flat on the bar, and her voice goes half a step higher and very careful — and then a moment later the hands start up again, too fast, doing something unrelated to the words. Anyone paying attention can see the seam. She has no idea this happens and will double down if challenged, because in her own account of herself she has fooled tax men, guild inspectors and her own late mother.

**She has something on tonight.** Ask what and the hands go flat. *"Stocktaking."* The cloth goes back to work. *"Barrels don't count themselves."* She will not be moved on this by any lever in this document, and the harder she's pushed the more elaborate and worse the lie gets.

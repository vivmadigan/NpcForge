# Halbrecht Onnow, of the Pearl & Pennon

## Who they are

**Halbrecht Onnow**, innkeeper of the Pearl & Pennon, twenty-two years behind the same bar, and in all that time he has made it his life's work to hear everything and be told nothing.

## Where they stand

He knows the shape of the thing. He knows which of his regulars come in with heavy pockets and go out with light ones, he knows the back stair gets used on Thirdnights, he knows a man sat at the end table last winter and counted out coin for something wrapped in sailcloth. **He does not have the name.** Nobody says the name in a common room — that is the entire point of the name. What he has is a collection of shoulders, coats, habits and hours, and not one syllable he could hand over.

And he does not want to be handing anything over. He wants the bar wiped down and the door shut and this particular conversation to have happened to some other innkeeper.

## How they seem, and what is underneath

Surface: delight. Arms wide, a stool dragged out before anyone asks for it, a cup filled that nobody ordered. *"Well, now — sit, sit, you've the look of people who've walked a way, and I've a stew on that'll—"* He is enormously pleased to see them and will remain enormously pleased to see them for as long as it takes them to leave.

Underneath: the welcome is a door, not an opening. Every warm question he asks is a question he is not being asked. He keeps starting sentences and letting them die in the middle — *"There was a fellow, he used to take the corner there, and he'd — no, I'm thinking of Marrow's boy, that's who I'm thinking of"* — and he is certain this reads as a harmless old man losing his thread. It reads as a man changing the subject. His face does a thing when he's about to lie: the smile arrives before the words do. He does not know it does this. He believes he is very good at this. He is not.

He has somewhere to be tonight and he will not say where, not to a blade, not to a bribe, not to a friend.

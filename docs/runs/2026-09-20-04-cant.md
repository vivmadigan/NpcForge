**What this run is for.** The third of step 7's three runs. By chance it drew the last unseen
obstacle kind, so runs 02–04 now cover `Wont`, `ForAPrice` and `Cant` — every branch of the
skill's per-obstacle rule, at the same difficulty, with the same four answers.

**The headline: `Cant` is the case a difficulty-only rule cannot survive, and the skill got it
right.** `SKILL.md` says *"**Cant** — they do not know, or may not say. No lever produces the
answer; a lever gets the players what this character can give instead."*

The output opens section 2 with *"Mara does not know the fence's name."* Every lever then
yields something else — a description, a meeting place, a courier — and lever 3 says so
outright: *"not the fence's name, which they do not possess."* The players leave with
"the red-gloved courier behind Saint Orra's market" instead.

A rule phrased as "at a `Wall`, withhold what the players want" would have produced a
character stonewalling over information they never had.

**Three runs, three structurally different scenes at one difficulty:**

| Run | Obstacle | What the scene is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 02 | `Wont` | Knows it, refuses it. Levers make her bend. |
| 03 | `ForAPrice` | Knows it, sells it. Names the price unprompted. |
| 04 | `Cant` | Does not know it. Levers redirect to what she has. |

That is [README "How difficulty works"](../../README.md) — *"Three completely different scenes
at the same difficulty"* — demonstrated end to end.

**Observed.**

- **Levers differ in content across all three runs**, which closes step 7's "Done when". The
  *fields* chosen are less varied: `Weakness` and `CharacterWants` appear in all three. Those
  are the two most lever-shaped entries in the brief, so this may be correct rather than lazy,
  but it is worth watching if a run ever needs variety from the other five.
- **Formatting drift.** Runs 02 and 03 wrote *Afterwards* as three labelled bullets. This run
  wrote it as one prose paragraph with the three cases embedded. All three cases are present,
  and `SKILL.md` does not demand bullets — but the fixed order is meant to be reliable, so this
  is the first sign the shape is a tendency rather than a guarantee. Same for the labels:
  `CharacterWants:` in runs 02–03 became `Character wants:` here.
- **The invented fields have now converged four times out of four.** Merrin Voss / Brass
  Lantern, Mara Venn / Brass Lantern, Mara Vell / Brass Kettle, **Mara Venn / Brass Lark** —
  an exact repeat of run 02's name. "Brass" is 4 for 4; "Mara" is 3 for 4. Nothing in the brief
  supplies either. This is the control group: rolled traits diverge, invented ones collapse
  onto the model's favourites, which is the whole argument the README makes for rolling.

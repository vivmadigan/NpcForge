**Observed.**

- **The name comes first, then the passage, then every part as before (A to G).** All eight parts,
  in order, on every sheet. Six used Markdown headings; E used bold labels. The passage is a block
  quote in all seven.
- **The room is the scene's.** The tiny bakery, the town's only inn, empty. No furniture, layout or
  other people the scene does not give. F calls it "the village's only inn", from the scene's
  "small village".
- **The look is the dice's, said plainly.** The age is in plain words (middle-aged, teenage), and
  B and F's adults have none. The paralysed part became "one arm held motionless" (C) and "his right
  arm hangs motionless" (G), stated, never the joke. D, the 20, shows both looks, and "multiple"
  stayed multiple: three tattoos and a baker's coat with croissant buttons.
- **The scene's gender won.** A and B rolled Female and the interview recorded Male from "his", so
  every sheet says he, and the namer named a man. C's interview missed the gender; its dice rolled
  Male, so nothing showed.
- **At their work.** Guarding croissants, dusting a tray, counting croissants, measuring one with a
  ruler, drying a cup, polishing a tankard. G's "tends to the inn" is the vaguest.
- **The line fits the attitude and the tone.** Comic lines in A to D and F, warm ones in E and G,
  and the mannerisms show: F's misused "bastion", G stepping too close, C crowding in.
- **One slip: A and B name the attitude in the line** ("I'm furious in alphabetical order", "even
  my anger has to queue"). The skill says the attitude shows in what they do and say, never as
  words about them. The scene itself says "furious", and both read as jokes. Left for the owner.
- **Nothing hidden reaches the passage.** E's "Where they stand" keeps `[what is really happening
  in the village]` as a gap, below the passage and not in it. No passage says or hints a want, a
  weakness or a position.
